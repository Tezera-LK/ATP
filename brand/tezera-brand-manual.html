01

Many pieces.
One event.

Overview

Tezera is a mobile app and web portal where Sri Lankan event organisers hire vendors and freelancers, and attendees find events and each other. Three kinds of people, three accounts, one app.

Story

Everything an event needs already exists: performers, venues, caterers, crews, audiences. It is scattered across chats, contacts and platforms. Tezera brings the pieces into one place and helps them fit.

The name

Tezera comes from tessera, a single tile in a mosaic. Alone it is a small fragment. Placed beside others it becomes the picture. The identity shows tiles fitting together, never jigsaw pieces.

“Tezera made it easy.”

Every screen, post and poster should leave people saying that.

Essence

An event pulls in dozens of people who have never worked together. Tezera puts them in one place, so planning one feels manageable instead of chaotic.

Personality

Confident, concise, clever, human, inviting and occasionally playful. A capable creative partner, never a corporate portal and never a toy.

Architecture

One shared system: typography, logo, geometry, components, icons, spacing. Each account changes only palette and emphasis.

04

Colour at work

Each account has a light and a dark mode. Primary carries actions and structure, secondary carries support and highlights, accent carries small moments of attention. Hexadecimal values are fixed and never substituted.

The vendor palette inverts the organiser's. The organiser's sage support colour leads for vendors, and the organiser's slate steps back to support. Organisers and vendors work as two sides of the same booking.

06

Icons built from tiles

Every icon sits on a 24-unit grid, drawn with 2-unit strokes, round joins and one filled tile as its anchor. The filled tile is the piece; the outline is the context it fits into.

07

The tile system

One soft, rounded tile. Laid in offset rows, the tiles fit together like bricks, and the gaps between them stay small and even. Leave a tile empty and it reads as a piece still to be connected. Repeat the tile, connect the rows, and let the pattern crop off the page.

Repeat: one tile, many times, with a few left open.

Connect: rows lock together edge to edge.

Assembled: a complete pattern.

Rules: keep one anchor tile per composition, let at least one edge crop off the page, and use negative space to show what has not yet connected.

08

Pieces finding where they belong

Motion moves pieces toward alignment: drift, snap, settle. Use a 600 to 900 millisecond ease-in-out curve and move one piece at a time. With reduced motion turned on, pieces appear in their final place without moving.

Screens follow the same rule. As a screen comes into view, its cards drift up and settle one after another, and progress fills in once. The phones in the next section show it.

09

Interface direction

Make a complex ecosystem feel simple: one clear action per screen, strong hierarchy, generous touch targets, visible focus and consistent component corners (large, large, large, small).

13

Do and don't

Do

  • Make complexity feel simple
  • Use the modular tile system
  • Connect tiles only where things relate
  • Keep the three accounts one family
  • Let each account keep its personality
  • Give primary, secondary and accent their own jobs
  • Balance play with sophistication
  • Start every layout from the tile

Don't

  • Look childish
  • Draw jigsaw puzzles
  • Make accounts feel like separate products
  • Introduce extra colours
  • Pile on trendy effects
  • Add geometry without a reason
  • Trade usability for experiments
  • Drift into corporate
