User personas

Version 1 · 15 October 2026 · based on the discovery surveys of 4–8 October 2026, the user stories and PRD version 5 · SDGP Agile Team Project

Eight people Tezera is built for: two consumers, two organisers, three vendors and freelancers, and one admin. Each one stands for a group the surveys heard from, and each is linked to the user stories written for them.

How to read these. Each persona combines several survey respondents. Names, ages and personal details are made up so the team can talk about "Nuwan" rather than "provider segment 1". They are marked (assumed) where the surveys don't support them. Goals, frustrations and evidence come from survey counts (out of 26 attendees, 7 organisers or 13 vendors and freelancers). Quotes are real survey answers, credited to the respondent who wrote them, who is not the persona.

Primary personas are the ones to design for first when two needs conflict. The fictional Sri Lankan names are spread across communities on purpose; they say nothing about language or preferences, which the surveys didn't ask about.

An undergraduate who goes to concerts, club nights and university events with the same group of friends. She hears about most events on Instagram or from friends, and the group makes its plans in a WhatsApp chat. Who else is going matters to her as much as who is playing.

… events never starting on time (not knowing the actual start time when the events are delayed, an estimated delay of starting time would be helpful with like live updates).

Attendee, several times a month, survey answer

Goals

  • Hear about events early, before tickets or places run out
  • See which friends are going and plan with them in one place
  • Find the entrance, toilets and her friends quickly at a big venue
  • Know straight away when an event is late, moved or cancelled

Frustrations

  • Missing events because she wasn't following the right page
  • Details released poster by poster, then hard to find again
  • Events starting late with no estimate and no update
  • No mobile signal at large venues, so nothing loads

Evidence

  • 25 of 26 attendees find events on Instagram; 22 through friends
  • 17 of 26 have missed an event because they heard too late
  • 20 of 26 say who else is attending matters, more than the line-up (18)
  • 14 of 26 coordinate in WhatsApp groups
  • Toilets (13), entrances and exits (10) are the hardest things to find
  • Delays and last-minute changes: the most common on-the-day complaint (6)

What this means for Tezera

  • Sharing out to Instagram and WhatsApp matters more than keeping her in the app
  • Friends going and the event group chat beat meeting strangers
  • Live changes must reach her by push, not only on the event page

The careful planner

A young professional who goes out less often and plans each outing: the date, the price, how to get there, what to wear and whether the event is real. He has paid for tickets to events that were later cancelled with no word on a refund, so he checks who is behind an event before he commits.

Sometimes authenticity is a problem specially in terms of purchasing tickets, because I'm sometimes skeptical whether I'm paying for the right agency or not, because some duplicates do exist (scams).

Attendee, several times a month, survey answer

Goals

  • Decide quickly from date, price, venue and line-up
  • Be sure the event and the organiser are real
  • Know the practical details: parking, dress code, rules, food prices
  • Get a straight answer from the organiser before he goes

Frustrations

  • Fake or duplicate ticket sellers
  • Cancelled events with no update and no refund
  • Helpline numbers nobody answers
  • Joining a WhatsApp group late and missing earlier answers

Evidence

  • Date and time (24 of 26), price (23) and venue (21) decide whether to go
  • 24 of 26 trust information from the official organiser account
  • In open answers, 5 asked about dress code and 3 about transport or parking
  • 6 of 26 trust reviews from previous attendees
  • Two attendees described cancelled events with no refund or update

What this means for Tezera

  • Verified badges and an 'unverified organiser' label carry his trust decision
  • The essentials sit at the top of the event page; nothing is buried in posts
  • Public Q&A answers stay visible to people who arrive late

Runs a small events company that puts on ticketed concerts and exhibitions with food and craft stalls. He fills stalls by phoning vendors he knows and tracks them in a spreadsheet, shares the layout through signs and staff on the day, and spends most of his time talking to vendors and promoting.

… that would be convenient to have everything in one place like places to find stalls people who are looking to sponsor and promote themselves, kinda like uber x social media for event organisers.

Organiser, 4–6 years, survey answer

Goals

  • Get people to engage with and share his events
  • Fill stalls without phone calls and spreadsheets
  • Keep attendees informed when something changes
  • See how much interest an event has, as it changes

Frustrations

  • Getting people to engage and share
  • Keeping information up to date in several places
  • Talking to vendors takes the most time
  • No easy way to share the venue layout before the day

Evidence

  • 6 of 7 organisers promote on Instagram; engagement and sharing is the top promotion difficulty (3)
  • All 7 coordinate the venue layout; only 1 shares a digital map
  • 6 of 7 manage stalls by contacting vendors directly; 2 use spreadsheets
  • Talking to vendors is the most time-consuming task for 5 of 7
  • 3 of 7 run more than 10 events a year

What this means for Tezera

  • Organisers work mainly on the web portal; the app covers on-the-day updates
  • Stalls, map pins and the event page share one stall number
  • Announcements must reach Going, Interested and followers in one send

Plans weddings and corporate events where the guests are invited, so she hardly promotes anything. Her job is hiring: photographers, decorators, sound and catering. She hires people she has used before or who come recommended, pays them in instalments, and has been let down by providers when it was too late to switch.

Sometimes people don't deliver according to our expectations and when we realise this, it's too late to switch to another person mostly because of budget and time constraints.

Organiser, 4–6 years, survey answer

Goals

  • Find reliable providers beyond her own contacts
  • Judge quality before hiring, from work and from other organisers
  • Compare quotes and agree terms in writing
  • Replace a provider quickly if one cancels

Frustrations

  • Work that doesn't meet expectations
  • Not knowing whether a new provider is reliable
  • Many small instalment payments and deadlines
  • Unclear pricing and quotes that are hard to compare

Evidence

  • All 7 organisers find providers through personal contacts and past vendors
  • Finding someone suitable (6 of 7) and quality not matching expectations (5) are the top hiring difficulties
  • 6 of 7 rate feedback from other organisers 5 out of 5 for usefulness
  • Availability decides for 5 of 7; getting quotes and unclear pricing trouble 3 each
  • One organiser described instalment payments as 'hard and time consuming'

What this means for Tezera

  • Whether the marketplace covers private events with no public page is still open (PRD section 14)
  • Reviews filtered by type of work serve her directly (R10, accepted: RV-08)
  • Payment terms and instalment tracking (R2, R3) are in PRD version 5 (HR-04, HR-08, HR-15)

The freelance photographer

Referrals, past clients, Instagram

A freelance photographer and videographer who gets almost all his work from referrals and repeat clients, and shows his work on Instagram. He quotes on the deliverables, so a vague brief means a wrong price. He has waited months to be paid and had jobs cancelled after a verbal yes.

The organizer wanting about 3 months to settle the payments. Settled the renting cost with personal funds and waited for the payment to come through.

Photographer and videographer, survey answer

Goals

  • Be found by organisers outside his network
  • Get the full brief, especially the deliverables, before quoting
  • Know the payment terms and the organiser's record before accepting
  • Build a track record that travels with him

Frustrations

  • Last-minute cancellations after a verbal confirmation
  • Late or missing payment
  • Briefs missing deliverables, timings and audience size
  • Negotiating prices

Evidence

  • 9 of 13 providers are photographers or videographers
  • 11 of 13 get work through referrals and 11 through past clients
  • Last-minute cancellations (9 of 13) and unpaid or late payment (7) are the top difficulties
  • 11 of 13 want payment terms before anything else about a new organiser
  • All 13 need date and location; deliverables are the most often missing detail

What this means for Tezera

  • Reviews run both ways and only from completed bookings
  • The brief stays attached to the booking as the written record
  • Payment tracking and late-cancellation records (R3, R4) are in PRD version 5 (HR-15, HR-16)

Runs a small food business that takes stalls at festivals, fairs and exhibitions. Organisers usually call her with an offer; she wants to know the stall fee, the expected crowd and whether the organiser can be trusted before she pays anything up front.

There should be a system to show the credibility of an event.

Media and design freelancer, survey answer

Goals

  • See open stall slots with type, fee and deadline
  • Check the organiser and the event are real before paying a stall fee
  • Be listed on the event page and the map so attendees find her
  • Earn reviews from attendees who visited her stall

Frustrations

  • Organisers who disappear without paying their vendors
  • Stall terms agreed by phone with nothing written down
  • Attendees who can't find her stall

Evidence

  • Thin: only 2 of 13 providers are caterers, so this persona leans on the organiser and attendee surveys
  • 6 of 7 organisers fill stalls by contacting vendors directly
  • 7 of 13 providers ask about the payment or stall fee before accepting
  • Attendees mentioned stalls running out (3) and asked about food prices (2)
  • One provider described an organiser who 'fled the venue' without paying vendors

What this means for Tezera

  • Only vendors take stalls; freelancers cannot apply
  • Stall fees are paid outside Tezera, so the stall record is the evidence in disputes
  • Her stall listing (items and prices, or her profile prices) shows on the interactive map, and attendees can search it by item (VM-10, VM-11)
  • Validate this persona in the follow-up interviews (survey analysis section 9)

Owns a sound and lighting company with a small crew. Each booking ties up equipment and people for a day, so he needs the run-of-show and the on-site technical details early, and a clash between bookings is expensive. He works outside Colombo, where organisers' own networks are thinner.

Organisers often share overall event concepts but omit on-site technical or logistical details until the event day.

Photographer, videographer and designer, survey answer

Goals

  • Get technical requirements and timings before quoting
  • Keep his calendar accurate so he is never double-booked
  • Reach organisers in other districts
  • Have terms in writing when an event changes on the day

Frustrations

  • Technical details missing until the event day
  • Scope creep during the event
  • Managing several bookings at once
  • Difficult organisers

Evidence

  • 4 of 13 offer sound and lighting and 4 music or DJ services
  • 3 of 13 need to know what equipment or facilities are provided
  • 2 of 13 find managing multiple bookings hard
  • 4 of 13 name difficult organisers and 4 unclear requirements
  • Only one provider works in Kandy and two islandwide, so evidence outside Colombo is thin

What this means for Tezera

  • The availability calendar fills itself from confirmed bookings
  • Districts served are a search filter, so providers outside Colombo show up
  • Equipment and run-of-show fields come with the structured brief (R1, accepted: HR-03, HR-05)

Works through the admin panel each day: checking verification documents, clearing the report queue, taking down fake events and settling disputes between organisers and providers. With money changing hands outside the app, the booking and stall record is all she has to go on.

Two-way verified reviews linked directly to completed jobs, clear dispute resolution before feedback is finalized, and objective evaluation metrics (punctuality, communication, deliverable quality, and payment speed).

Photographer, videographer and designer, describing what a fair system needs, survey answer

Goals

  • Verify real organisers and providers quickly and consistently
  • Remove fake events before people pay outside the app
  • Settle disputes from the written record, not from claims
  • Keep ratings and rank scores free of fake reviews

Frustrations

  • Disputes with no written terms to check (anticipated)
  • Personal documents she must handle under the PDPA (anticipated)
  • Reports arriving from many places with no single queue (anticipated)

Evidence

  • No admin was surveyed. Her work comes from PRD 6.10 (AD-01 to AD-06)
  • 24 of 26 attendees trust the official organiser account, so verification carries trust
  • Providers asked for fair, two-way reviews with a dispute step

What this means for Tezera

  • NIC and registration documents open only for admins, through short-lived signed links
  • Every admin action is logged (AD-06)
  • Hidden reviews and banned accounts drop out of rank scores automatically

Who we are not designing for in the ATP release

  • The in-app ticket buyer. Attendees who want to buy tickets, pick seats and get in with a QR code. Ticketing is the first phase after the ATP, so the ATP release links out to the organiser's own ticket seller instead (US-C16, US-C17 are Later).
  • The stranger-meetup seeker. Only 1 of 26 attendees asked for a way to find new people to go with, and 7 don't interact with other attendees at all. Event boards stay, but the design favours friends and the event group chat.
  • The sponsor hunter. 4 of 7 organisers coordinate sponsors, but finding sponsors is not in the PRD. It is parked for a later phase (survey analysis section 8).
