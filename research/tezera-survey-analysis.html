What organisers, providers and attendees told us

Analysis of three online surveys run 4–8 October 2026 · written 8 October 2026 · SDGP Agile Team Project

We surveyed the three groups Tezera serves: people who attend events, people who organise them, and the vendors and freelancers they hire. The answers back most of the problems the PRD assumed. They also show that providers worry most about late payment and cancelled jobs, which the PRD only partly covers.

Key findings

  1. People find events on Instagram, and Tezera has to work alongside it. 25 of 26 attendees discover events on Instagram and 22 through friends. Organisers promote there too (6 of 7). Tezera won't replace Instagram, so event pages need to be easy to share into it and into WhatsApp.
  2. Attendees miss events and hunt for details. 17 of 26 have missed an event at least sometimes because they heard too late or lacked information. Their most common complaint is that details are spread across posts, group chats and websites.
  3. Trust comes from an official, verified organiser. 24 of 26 attendees trust event information when it comes from the official organiser account. One described duplicate ticket sellers; another lost money on two cancelled events with no update and no refund.
  4. Finding things at a venue is a common problem, and few organisers share a map. 18 of 26 attendees have had trouble finding something at an event, toilets and entrances most of all. Only 1 of 7 organisers shares a digital map; most rely on signs and staff.
  5. Hiring runs on personal networks, and the risk is quality. All 7 organisers find providers through personal contacts and vendors they've used before. Their main problems are finding someone suitable (6) and work that doesn't meet expectations (5).
  6. Providers' biggest problems are cancellations and late payment. 9 of 13 vendors and freelancers name last-minute cancellations and 7 name unpaid or late payments. 11 of 13 want to know an organiser's payment terms before taking a job.
  7. Both sides want reviews, as long as they are fair. 18 of the 20 organisers and providers rated feedback from past jobs 4 or 5 out of 5 for usefulness. Providers want reviews in both directions, tied to real jobs, with a way to dispute them.

Limits of this data

  • Small, friendly samples. Responses came from people the team knows, not a random sample. Treat percentages as signals, not measurements of the Sri Lankan market. Seven organisers is enough to spot shared problems but too few to rank them.
  • Some people answered twice. Six people answered both the attendee and provider surveys, and one person answered all three. That is why 46 responses come from 39 people. Each survey is analysed on its own.
  • Colombo-heavy. Nearly every organiser and provider works mainly in Colombo, so we know little about other districts.
  • Multiple choice. Most questions allowed several answers, so the bars in each chart add up to more than the number of people.
  • Open answers were coded by hand. Counts of themes from open answers (for example "6 attendees mentioned delays") are our own grouping of what people wrote, and could be grouped differently.
  • Privacy. The raw responses include email addresses, so they are kept out of the repository. Quotes here are anonymous, trimmed where marked with "…", and otherwise as written.

The other problems at events, from open answers:

  • Delays and last-minute changes (6 attendees): events starting late with no estimate, cancellations without updates.
  • Queues and crowding (4): long entrance queues, ticket-counter lines, stalls overwhelmed.
  • Stalls running out (3): food and water selling out halfway through, after a ticket that promised them.
  • Losing friends in the crowd (1), made worse by poor mobile signal at large venues.
  • Organisers who can't be reached (2): helpline numbers that don't answer, or joining the WhatsApp group too late to see earlier messages.

Hiring stays inside personal networks. When a provider's work falls short, organisers often find out too late to switch. Organisers judge new providers by recommendations, portfolios, past event experience and a personal meeting. Reviews and portfolios on a profile (RV-03, AC-04) cover the first three; the in-app chat (MS-02) is the closest substitute for the meeting.

Asked what feedback should tell them, organisers named quality of work (3), meeting deadlines and time management (2), reliability (2), communication and attitude (1) and price (1). These match the PRD's review categories: quality, punctuality, communication and value for money. One organiser wanted to filter feedback by the type of work.

Sometimes people don't deliver according to our expectations and when we realise this, it's too late to switch to another person mostly because of budget and time constraints.

Asked what would make a review system fair, providers said:

  • reviews go both ways, and only from real, completed jobs (2 answers);
  • a range of reviews, good and bad, not just praise (2);
  • a dispute step before a review is final, and objective categories such as punctuality, communication, quality of deliverables and payment speed (1);
  • anonymous reviews (1). This conflicts with tying reviews to bookings, because the other side can usually guess who wrote one.

Two-way verified reviews linked directly to completed jobs, clear dispute resolution before feedback is finalized, and objective evaluation metrics (punctuality, communication, deliverable quality, and payment speed).

7What this means for Tezera

Most of the PRD stands. The research backs the hiring marketplace, two-way reviews, verified badges, stall applications, venue maps, announcements and event Q&A. The changes below are proposals for the group to review. The PRD has not been changed yet. Each change has a matching user story in the user story document.

Priorities are proposed. R1, R2, R7 and R9 extend existing Must requirements rather than adding new features.

What the research confirms

  • Verification (AC-08, AC-09) is how Tezera earns trust: attendees trust official accounts, and providers want to know who they are working for.
  • Two-way reviews tied to completed jobs (6.5) are what providers asked for, almost word for word, including a "Payment" category for organisers.
  • Stall applications in the app (HR-10 to HR-12) replace phone calls and spreadsheets.
  • Venue maps (VM-01 to VM-04) fill a gap: only one organiser shares a map today.
  • Friends going (CM-05) and the event group chat (MS-04) fit how attendees already plan, with friends and in WhatsApp groups.
  • Public Q&A (MS-06) keeps answers visible to people who join late, unlike a WhatsApp group.

9Next research steps

  1. Interview two or three of the organisers who answered. The PRD planned interviews, and seven survey answers can't show how organisers would use the hiring flow.
  2. Test R1 to R4 with providers: show five or six of them a mock booking request with structured brief and payment terms, and ask what's missing.
  3. Reach beyond Colombo and beyond students: attendees outside university age, and providers in other districts.
  4. Ask about ticketing directly before the ticketing phase: what organisers use now and what they pay for it.
  5. Usability-test the prototype against the PRD's success metric: publish an event, post a job, upload a venue map.
