1In-app payments to vendors and freelancers: is it legal in Sri Lanka?

Question
Can Tezera let organisers pay vendors and freelancers (deposits, instalments, stall fees, refunds) inside the app, first as a student project and later for real? What would it take to do it legally in Sri Lanka?
Method
Web research by a Claude Code research agent on 15 October 2026; read-only. Sources: CBSL regulations and gazettes, gateway help and developer pages, law-firm and tax-adviser articles, news reports. Secondary sources are labelled as such.
Date
15 October 2026

Not legal advice

This is desk research by a software team, not advice from a lawyer, accountant or the Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL). It is enough to choose a safe design for the ATP. Before Tezera moves real money, a Sri Lankan lawyer and accountant must check the open risks listed below.

Bottom line

  1. Sandbox payments now are fine. Test-mode payments move no real money. Nothing was found in CBSL rules that stops a student team integrating a gateway's sandbox. The PayHere sandbox is a free self sign-up; Genie Business gives sandbox credentials on request by email.
  2. Holding or splitting real money most likely needs CBSL registration. Collecting organisers' money and paying it on to vendors matches CBSL's "Money or Value Transfer Service" (MVTS). Registration needs a Companies Act company with at least LKR 15m unimpaired capital that does only MVTS business. That is not realistic for a student or start-up platform.
  3. No Sri Lankan gateway we checked documents marketplace or split payouts. PayHere's help pages list "Multi-vendor platform" as unsupported because of "payment regulations & partner bank policies". Stripe and Stripe Connect are not available to Sri Lankan businesses.
  4. For a real launch, Tezera should never touch the money. Either (a) each provider has their own gateway merchant account (PayHere, Genie or WEBXPAY), Tezera produces a payment link or hosted checkout for that provider, the money settles to them, and Tezera invoices its commission separately; or (b) Tezera records the agreement and its status only, and payment happens outside the app (the current ATP decision).
  5. A real launch also needs a business behind it. A registered business, tax registration, and readiness for the core duties of the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) from 1 January 2027.

1Platform holding money (payment facilitator)

  • CBSL MVTS Regulations No. 1 of 2024 cover the "acceptance of cash… or other stores of value and the payment of a corresponding sum… to a beneficiary… [which] may involve… a final payment to a third party". An MVTS provider needs a CBSL Certificate of Registration. Registration covers "safekeeping of funds to the extent necessary", which is the closest thing to escrow. Entities licensed or authorised by another regulator are exempt.
    Source: CBSL, MVTS Regulations No. 1 of 2024 (PDF)
  • 2025 amendment (Regulations No. 1 of 2025, Gazette 2468/06, in effect 31 December 2025). The definition covers transfers "within Sri Lanka"; cross-border transfers are subject to the Foreign Exchange Act No. 12 of 2017. Eligibility: a Companies Act company, at least LKR 15m unimpaired capital, and "exclusively" MVTS business. Unlicensed operators had to apply by 31 March 2026.
    Source: CBSL, MVTS (Amendment) Regulations No. 1 of 2025 (PDF)
  • Escrow beyond that. A registered MVTS provider may not accept deposits without a separate licence (reg. 12). Holding money for a long time might be caught by the Banking Act or the Finance Business Act (agent's reading, not verified).
  • Payment Cards and Mobile Payment Systems Regulations No. 1 of 2013 license card issuers, financial acquirers, mobile payment operators and e-money operators. CBSL lists no separate "payment facilitator", "aggregator" or "IPG" licence.
    Sources: CBSL, payments and settlements systems; Gazette 1813/25 (PDF)
  • Payment and Settlement Systems Act No. 28 of 2005: only CBSL-authorised entities may operate a payment system.
  • CBSL Policy Agenda (8 January 2026): "legal reforms relating to payment and settlement systems are expected to be undertaken in 2026".
    Source: CBSL, Central Bank's policy agenda for 2026 and beyond (PDF)
  • Financial Transactions Reporting Act No. 6 of 2006: a registered MVTS provider is probably a reporting institution (not verified).
    Source: Financial Intelligence Unit, establishment
  • Foreign Exchange Act: only matters for non-residents or foreign currency.

3Students and a real launch

  • Sandbox: the PayHere sandbox sign-up states no registration requirement. Genie gives sandbox access on request.
  • Live: PayHere and the bank gateways need a business and a bank account. Genie reportedly accepts an NIC.
  • VAT: sources disagree. De Saram says the threshold dropped to LKR 36m a year (LKR 9m a quarter) from 1 July 2026, and that VAT on digital services from non-residents started then. ACCA (July 2026) says the cut is "expected soon". Check with the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) (not verified).
    Sources: De Saram, Sri Lanka VAT changes 2026; ACCA AB magazine, threshold changes
  • Social Security Contribution Levy (SSCL): 2.5%. The threshold fell to LKR 36m a year (LKR 9m a quarter) from 1 July 2026 under the SSCL (Amendment) Act No. 10 of 2026.
    Source: Taxable.lk, SSCL amendment 2026
  • Withholding tax (WHT): since 3 June 2026, 5% is withheld on service fees paid to resident individuals where the monthly total is over LKR 100,000. The list includes photographers, artists and musicians. Inland Revenue (Amendment) Act No. 11 of 2026, IRD notice SEC/PN/IT/2026/02.
    Source: RegFollower, Sri Lanka tax law changes
  • Who withholds (the organiser or the platform) depends on who is legally the payer. A lawyer or accountant needs to confirm this.

4Data protection and card security

  • PDPA No. 9 of 2022, as amended by Act No. 22 of 2025. Gazette 2498/16 (22 July 2026) brings ss. 2–3, Part I (processing) and Part III (controllers and processors: data protection officer, breach notice, impact assessments) into force on 1 January 2027. Part II (rights) and Part VII (penalties) are awaiting a date.
    Sources: Daily FT; Neelakandan & Neelakandan; The Morning
  • Data leaving Sri Lanka (AWS Mumbai). The 2025 amendment replaced the adequacy test. Data can go abroad if the processing complies with the Act and uses safeguards the Data Protection Authority specifies, or on grounds such as explicit consent or contractual necessity. The Authority's safeguard directive has not been published yet (not verified), so Tezera should document a basis for hosting in Mumbai.
    Source: Concentric AI (vendor blog)
  • Bank and payment data: whether it is a "special category" under the PDPA is not verified.
  • PCI DSS. A redirect or fully outsourced payment page gets the shortest self-assessment (SAQ A). An embedded iframe must also meet the script-attack protections (requirements 6.4.3 and 11.6.1) from 1 April 2025. In-app SDK checkouts: not verified.
    Source: PCI Security Standards Council, SAQ A eligibility FAQ

5Contracts and consumer law

  • E-contracts are enforceable under the Electronic Transactions Act No. 19 of 2006, amended by Act No. 25 of 2017 (which adopts the UN electronic communications convention).
    Sources: Act No. 25 of 2017 (PDF); De Saram, validity of electronic signatures
  • Consumer Affairs Authority (CAA) Gazette 2332/15 (17 May 2023) applies to e-commerce entities and platform operators: no misleading practices; fair rating and review systems; a privacy statement; an order review step before confirming; receipts showing price, payment method and delivery terms; cooling-off and cancellation rights. Whether it covers organiser-to-provider (business-to-business) deals is not verified. A CAA e-commerce reform is under way.
    Sources: De Saram, new regulations for online sellers; EconomyNext, e-commerce consumer protection
  • Platform liability when a vendor doesn't deliver: no specific Sri Lankan rule was found. General contract law and Tezera's own terms apply.

6CBSL changes 2024–2026

  • MVTS Regulations 2024 and the 2025 amendment (section 1 above).
  • LANKAQR Specification v1.3 (Circular 02/2026, 13 March 2026, addendum 17 June 2026): merchant QR and person-to-person payments, for financial institutions, e-money operators "or any other third party".
  • JustPay limits and fees (Circular 01/2026). Customer identification for payment apps (Circular 02/2024).
  • None of these target marketplaces.
    Source: CBSL, directions, circulars and guidelines on payments and settlements

Open risks for a lawyer

Questions the research could not settle. They must be answered before Tezera moves real money.

  1. Does collecting money as the provider's agent fall outside MVTS? No commercial-agent exemption is listed.
  2. Would a "Tezera Pay" need its own company, given the rule that an MVTS company does only MVTS business?
  3. Does PayHere's multi-vendor ban apply even if each provider has their own account and Tezera only takes a commission? Ask PayHere, Genie and WEBXPAY directly.
  4. Who withholds tax and issues the invoice: the organiser or the platform?
  5. Which VAT threshold is current?
  6. What PDPA transfer safeguards are needed for AWS Mumbai, and what are the data protection officer duties from 1 January 2027?
  7. Do the CAA rules apply when the organiser is an individual?
  8. Will the 2026 payments-law reform add an aggregator licence?

What the PRD did with this

Decided by Damian on 15 October 2026; in PRD version 6, section 6.4 (Hiring marketplace). Ticketing (TK-*) and ticket refunds stay Later.

  • Sandbox only in the ATP. No real money moves. Every payment screen and receipt is labelled "Test payment, no real money".
  • Direct to the provider; Tezera never holds funds. Each provider (and each organiser, for stall fees) connects their own PayHere sandbox merchant account. Tezera opens that account's hosted checkout on the web or the PayHere mobile SDK in the app, and the money settles straight to the payee. Tezera never holds, splits or pays out money, so the same design stays outside MVTS for a real launch.
  • PayHere behind an adapter. The gateway is coded behind a small payment-provider interface so Genie Business or WEBXPAY can be added later.
  • Card details only on PayHere's hosted page (PCI DSS SAQ A scope). PayHere's payment notifications reach one small HTTPS endpoint, a normal REST route in the NestJS API (PRD version 7 dropped the separate Lambda function URL; the route is planned, not built), which checks PayHere's signature and ignores repeats of the same order ID. Merchant secrets stay server-side (proposed).
  • Fallback: a payee without a connected account is paid outside the app, and the organiser marks the instalment received by hand (HR-15 stays the base path).
  • ShouldHR-14 (was Later): organiser pays a booking's deposit and instalments in the app through the provider's connected account. A successful payment marks the instalment received (HR-15); both sides get a receipt.
  • ShouldHR-17: organisers and providers connect a payment account (their own PayHere sandbox merchant account) on their profile.
  • ShouldHR-18: a vendor pays a stall fee through the organiser's connected account; payment confirms the stall.
  • ShouldHR-19: cancellation fees and refunds per the booking's cancellation terms (HR-08, HR-16), through the gateway (sandbox).
  • ShouldHR-20: payment history and receipts for both sides, with Tezera's commission as a separate line (rate proposed, not charged in the ATP); admins see payment records for disputes.

PRD section 14 (open questions) lists the questions to settle before real money moves: the eight open risks above.

2Map builder: what we can take from ai-studio

Question
Damian's own repo, ai-studio, turns an uploaded image into an editable structured result. Which of its ideas and code can Tezera reuse for a venue map builder that gives every event map the same standard format?
Method
Web research by a Claude Code research agent on 15 October 2026; read-only. The agent read the repo's README, plans, specs and source files on GitHub.
Repo
github.com/EnderGuardian25/ai-studio, default branch v2

Licence. The repo has no LICENSE file (the GitHub API reports license: null), so by default nobody else may copy it. Its owner is Damian, who is on the Tezera team, and he allows the code to be copied and built on (15 October 2026). The licence risk is therefore moot for Tezera.

What the repo is

The README calls it "bistec-studio": an internal Bistec marketing tool that "turns a brief into a finished, on-brand, ready-to-publish social media post for Instagram and LinkedIn". Claude writes HTML and CSS, either by filling a brand template (Path A) or by designing freely (Path B), and Puppeteer renders the result to PNG.

  • Stack: Next.js 16, React 19, TypeScript, PostgreSQL with Prisma, MinIO, better-auth, puppeteer-core, @anthropic-ai/sdk, openai, a Gemini image provider, Docker Compose, Playwright, Vitest.
  • Other features: brand kits, campaigns and scheduling, draft revisions, inline editing, publishing to Instagram, LinkedIn and WhatsApp, and an MCP server.

It is not a map tool. There is no floor-plan, venue, map or zone code; no canvas library (Konva and Fabric are absent); no grid snapping; no JSON shape schema; no layers; and no region detection. What it offers is a set of mechanisms that carry over.

Mechanisms worth copying

AImage to editable structured template ("F6")

An admin uploads an image. It is saved as a REFERENCE_IMAGE artifact, so the source is kept and can be regenerated. It goes to Claude vision as base64 image blocks; the model returns HTML with named slots filled with sample content. The result is returned, not saved: the admin must fix it in the editor and then save. The prompt asks for "clear content SLOTS: a headline, supporting body text, a logo area, and a primary photo/subject slot". The plan says: "Vision output HTML may be imperfect — the editor step is the safety net (keep it mandatory)."

src/lib/aspectRatio.ts: nearestAspectRatio(w,h) picks the closest of three fixed canvas sizes, and the admin can override it. This is the nearest thing to calibrating a scale.

Files: docs/plans/feature-6-image-to-template.md, src/lib/brandkit/templateFromImage.ts, src/app/api/admin/brandkits/[id]/templates/from-image/route.ts, src/lib/agent/vision.ts

BClick to select and edit

The output is shown in a sandboxed iframe (sandbox="allow-same-origin", no scripts). A click records { path: number[], tag, text: fingerprint, baseRevisionNumber }. The server finds the element again by its path, checks the fingerprint, and refuses if the match is ambiguous ("fail closed"). Every save is a new revision.

Files: src/components/drafts/inlineElementEdit.ts, src/lib/drafts/htmlLocator.ts, spec docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-23-draft-inline-edit-design.md

CMeasuring rendered layout

Chromium reads the laid-out boxes and computed styles of the rendered output.

File: src/lib/renderer/domFacts.ts

DUntrusted image content

The vision prompt tells the model that text inside an uploaded image is data, not instructions.

File: src/lib/agent/vision.ts

How it maps to Tezera

  1. Upload a PNG or JPG floor plan. It is stored in S3 and kept as the source.
  2. Calibrate: the organiser marks two points and enters the real distance between them; positions are stored in normalised 0–1 coordinates. This must be built; ai-studio's aspect-ratio snapping is too crude.
  3. AI draft (Could): Claude on Amazon Bedrock returns JSON boxes {id, kind: zone|stall|pin, label, x, y, w, h}. It is a draft only.
  4. Editor on the web portal. This must be built (Konva or plain SVG, with grid snapping).
  5. Assign vendors to stalls from a side panel. Elements keep stable IDs, like ai-studio's path and fingerprint.
  6. Publish as versioned map JSON (like ai-studio's revisions) in PostgreSQL as a jsonb document (proposed, PRD version 7 section 8; planned, not built), drawn as SVG over the image in the mobile app and on the web. This feeds the consumer map features VM-08 to VM-12.

Reusable, and what we must build

Reusable from ai-studio

  • Keep the source image.
  • AI output is an editable draft, checked by a person.
  • Snapping.
  • Stable element IDs, with stale-edit checks that fail closed.
  • Every publish is a new revision.
  • The prompt guard for untrusted image text.

Must be built for Tezera

  • The canvas or box editor.
  • Scale calibration.
  • The map JSON schema.
  • A map renderer for both the app and the web portal.
  • PDF to image conversion (the repo only uses pdf-parse to extract text).

Risks

  • Licence: moot, because Damian owns the repo and allows copying.
  • Paid AI and keys. ai-studio uses an Anthropic API key or Claude CLI OAuth, optionally OpenAI or Gemini, and stores users' Claude tokens. Bedrock fits Tezera's AWS stack better.
  • Heavy dependencies that don't fit Tezera's stack: Puppeteer and Chromium, MinIO, Docker. (PostgreSQL with Prisma is now Tezera's main database too, in the NestJS API on the dev branch.) Adapt the ideas; don't lift the code wholesale.
  • Accuracy on Sri Lankan plans. How well an AI draft works on scanned or hand-drawn floor plans was not researched. So the AI draft stays Could, and drawing by hand is Must.

Map format example proposed

An illustrative shape for the standard Tezera map JSON (proposed). Positions and sizes are fractions of the background, from 0 to 1, so the same map draws correctly at any screen size. A pin is a point, so its width and height are 0. The final schema will be set when the builder is designed.

{
  "version": 3,
  "background": { "kind": "image", "src": "maps/evt_123/background.png", "widthM": 60, "heightM": 40 },
  "scale": { "a": [0.10, 0.90], "b": [0.40, 0.90], "distanceM": 18 },
  "items": [
    { "id": "z1", "kind": "zone",  "label": "Food court", "x": 0.05, "y": 0.10, "w": 0.40, "h": 0.30 },
    { "id": "s7", "kind": "stall", "label": "Stall 7",    "x": 0.08, "y": 0.14, "w": 0.06, "h": 0.05,
      "zoneId": "z1", "stallNo": 7, "vendorId": "ven_42" },
    { "id": "p1", "kind": "pin",   "label": "Toilets",    "x": 0.92, "y": 0.50, "w": 0, "h": 0 }
  ]
}

background.kind is image, pdf (first page used) or grid (blank grid, no src). scale holds the two marked points and the real distance between them (VM-17). zoneId, stallNo and vendorId are optional.

What the PRD did with this

Decided by Damian on 15 October 2026; in PRD version 6, section 6.6 (Venue maps). VM-01 (Must) is unchanged, except that the uploaded PNG or JPG becomes the builder's background layer.

  • The builder runs on the web portal. The mobile app does quick on-the-day edits: move, rename, hide.
  • One standard, versioned map format. Every map, however it starts (image, PDF page or blank grid), is saved in the same Tezera map JSON. Each publish is a new version.
  • AI draft on Claude via Amazon Bedrock. It stays inside AWS (IAM, no separate key). It is a draft only; the organiser checks it before anything publishes, and text inside the image is treated as data, not instructions. Whether Bedrock offers Claude in ap-south-1 (Mumbai) is not verified; it may need cross-region inference.
  • MustVM-16: map builder on the web portal: draw, move, resize, label and delete zones, stalls and pins; grid snapping, alignment, number a row of stalls in one step, duplicate, undo. Quick edits in the app.
  • MustVM-17: set the scale by marking two points and entering the real distance (or by grid size), so stall sizes show in metres.
  • MustVM-18: every map is stored in one standard, versioned format; each publish is a new version, and the organiser can restore an earlier one.
  • ShouldVM-19: upload a PDF floor plan; the first page becomes the background.
  • ShouldVM-20: start from a blank grid of a set size (e.g. 60 m × 40 m) with no floor plan.
  • ShouldVM-21: save a map as a reusable venue map and start a later event's map from it.
  • CouldVM-22: AI draft: Tezera suggests zones, stalls and pins from the uploaded plan as an editable draft; nothing publishes until the organiser checks it.
