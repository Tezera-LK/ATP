tezera

Version 6 · updated 15 October 2026 · first written 30 September 2026 by Navindu Ranwatte

Tezera is a mobile app and web portal that brings Sri Lanka's event organisers, vendors, freelancers and consumers onto one platform. Organisers promote events, hire vendors and freelancers, fill stalls and share venue maps; consumers discover events, mark that they're going and meet up through forums. The ATP release covers all four core modules: events and promotion, the hiring marketplace, community and venue maps. Ticketing and entry come first after the ATP, followed by payments in the app; premium accounts come in a later version.

  • Serverless on AWS Amplify (section 8): the backend moves to AWS Amplify Gen 2 in ap-south-1 (Mumbai). Amplify Data (a GraphQL API on AWS AppSync, with subscriptions for live updates) replaces NestJS with REST and SSE; Amazon DynamoDB replaces PostgreSQL; Lambda functions triggered by SQS, DynamoDB Streams and EventBridge Scheduler replace Redis and the BullMQ worker; Amazon Cognito handles sign-in; Amplify Storage (S3 and CloudFront) replaces Cloudinary; Amazon SES replaces Resend; the web portal moves from Vercel to Amplify Hosting.   • Dependencies (section 12): an AWS account is added; email and media rows updated. No requirements change.

1. Problem and opportunity

We believe organising a large event in Sri Lanka means juggling separate channels, and no single place connects organisers, vendors, freelancers and attendees. These are hypotheses, not findings: the team will test them during discovery, before the design is final.

PartyProblem we believe they faceHow we will check
OrganisersVendors and freelancers are found through word of mouth and social media, with little trustworthy reputation data. Ticketing, promotion and attendee updates each need a different tool.Interviews with event organisers
VendorsHearing about events that need their services, or open stall slots, depends on personal contacts. Good work at one event does not build a visible track record.Survey of stall operators and agencies
FreelancersThe same as vendors. Individuals also find it harder to compete with agencies without portfolios and reviews in one place.Survey of photographers, videographers, DJs and MCs
ConsumersEvent details are spread across organisers' social pages, venue layouts are rarely shared ahead of time, and there is no easy way to find others who are going.Consumer survey
v3 Survey results are in. Surveys of 26 attendees, 7 organisers and 13 vendors and freelancers ran from 4 to 8 October 2026. The findings, and the PRD changes they suggest, are in the survey analysis for the group to review.
v5 All twelve recommended changes were accepted on 15 October 2026 and are now in the requirements below (see "What changed in version 5").

The opportunity: one platform where each party's activity feeds the others. Events create demand for vendors and freelancers. Completed jobs and attended events create reviews. Forum activity builds an audience for the organiser's next event.

2. Goals and non-goals

The ATP release should prove that one platform can carry the work around an event: promotion, hiring, venue maps and community. Ticketing follows as the first phase after the ATP.

Goals

  • Organisers publish events and promote them with posts and reels; consumers mark events as 'Going' or 'Interested'. v2
  • Organisers hire vendors and freelancers by direct booking or job posts, and fill stalls by invitation or open application.
  • Reputation is visible and earned: reviews run in four directions and feed category ratings, a rank score, tier badges and leaderboards.
  • Consumers plan their visit with the organiser's venue map and meet others through event boards, communities and meetups.
  • Conversations between organisers, consumers, vendors and freelancers happen in the app instead of across separate tools.

Non-goals for the MVP

  • Ticketing and entry: ticket tiers, reserved seats, free RSVP tickets, QR tickets and gate scanning, and refunds. This is the first phase after the ATP (6.3). v2
  • Payments of any kind in the app. Bookings and stalls are agreed in the app but paid outside it; in-app payments would come in a later phase, if the group takes the idea further. v2
  • Premium accounts for VIP or fast-sale tickets (later version).
  • A 3D view of the venue; the interactive 2D map (6.6) covers finding stalls, stages and facilities. Google Maps directions to the venue are still under team discussion (section 14). v2 v5
  • Posts or reels from vendors, freelancers or consumers in the main feed. The feed is for organisers; providers show their work in portfolios.
  • More than one role on a single account.

5. Competitive landscape

We found no platform, Sri Lankan or global, that combines ticketing, vendor and freelancer hiring, and community in one product. Local ticketing sites stop at tickets, and local vendor directories stop at listings.

PlatformMarketWhat it offersNot found, compared with our hub
MyTicketsSri LankaTicket marketplace for concerts, sports, movies and transport; seat-specific tickets; an organiser console for sales and check-insVendor hiring, reviews, community
TicketsMinistrySri LankaTicket tiers and early-bird pricing; a QR scanner app with signed single-use codes and an offline mode; seat maps set up by its teamVendor hiring, reviews; community is a WhatsApp channel

BookMyShow LKSri Lanka, since 2016Movie, event and sports tickets; organiser sales tracking and gate entryHiring; reviews cover movies only
WeddingArcade, Plan A Wedding, WedMeGoodSri LankaWedding vendor listings (photographers, decorators, DJs), quote requests, and ratings and reviews on WedMeGoodPublic events, ticketing, community
DJBooking.lkSri LankaVerified DJs, with packages to compare and bookOther provider types, ticketing
ikman.lk Events & EntertainmentSri LankaClassified ads for photography, rentals and entertainersReviews, a booking flow, ticketing
EventenyUSTicketing with QR check-in, vendor and exhibitor applications, booth assignment, and maps where attendees find vendorsFreelancer hiring, forums, direct messages, meetups
PoshUSSocial ticketing: follow friends and organisers, share events to group chats, scannable ticketsVendor hiring
EventbriteGlobalCreate, promote and sell event tickets, including reserved seatingVendor hiring, forums
MeetupGlobalInterest-based communities that host events; paid plans can charge event feesSeating, vendor hiring

Eventeny is the closest match: it handles vendors and maps, but not freelancers or community. "Not found" means absent from the pages checked, not proven missing. Local rivals already offer QR entry and seat selection, so those are table stakes; the case for Tezera rests on hiring, reputation and community in one place. v2 That is also why the ATP release can leave ticketing out: it is not what sets Tezera apart, and it comes first after the ATP.

Proposed rank score (a starting point to tune with real data):

Rank = 0.5 · R̂ − 14 + 0.2 · V + 0.2 · B + 0.1 · S R̂ = v·R + m·Cv + m

R is the average star rating and v the number of reviews. C is the average rating across the category, and m is a minimum review count (proposed: 5). Together they stop a single five-star review from topping a leaderboard. V is the review count v, and B the number of completed jobs, each scaled 0-1 against the category leader. S is the share of booking requests answered. For organisers (proposed), B counts completed events and R combines ratings from consumers and providers. C and the scaling use all organisers, and S is the share of consumer chats and Q&A questions answered.

The team runs no servers. The architecture diagram is in prd/tezera-architecture.html.

Ranking, the feed and search v4 v6

  • Rank score (RV-04, HR-01). Stored as a rankScore attribute on provider and organiser profiles. A Lambda function started by DynamoDB Streams recalculates it whenever a review is written, or hidden by moderation (RV-07).
  • Leaderboards and search order (RV-06, HR-01). A GSI with category#district as its partition key and rankScore as its sort key. The top of a board is one query read in descending order, and provider search uses the same index, so results come sorted by rank. The board of top organisers uses an index with one fixed partition (proposed; fine at ATP scale).
  • Tier badges (RV-05). Worked out by the same Lambda from the rank score and completed jobs or events, and stored with the profile.
  • Promotion feed (EV-03, EV-04). Built when the user opens it: posts from each organiser they follow, read through a GSI on (authorId, createdAt), merged newest first and paged with cursors. Only organisers post, so there are few authors. If follow lists grow long, switch to writing each new post into its followers' feeds (fan-out on write) from DynamoDB Streams.
  • Trending posts. A scheduled Lambda scores recent posts by engagement that fades with age, (likes + 2·comments + 3·shares) / (age in hours + 2)^1.5, every 15 minutes, and stores the top list as one item (formula and interval proposed; tune with real data).
  • Likes and view counts. Each like is its own item keyed by post and user, written with a condition so nobody likes twice. Counts on the post are kept with atomic counter updates; view counts are added in batches (proposed). These counts also feed the organiser analytics (OD-04).
  • Search (EV-05, HR-01). GSIs for category, district and date, with price and free-event filters, plus prefix matching on event and provider names. There is no typo tolerance in the ATP.

Upgrade paths, if the pilot needs them v4 v6

  • Search: Algolia or Typesense Cloud, kept in sync by a Lambda on DynamoDB Streams, if prefix search is not good enough. Both tolerate typos. Amplify can also stream DynamoDB into Amazon OpenSearch Service, but OpenSearch costs money every month even when idle.
  • Recommendations (EV-07, Could): a scheduled Lambda builds a list for each user from interests, followed organisers and district. Amazon Personalize only at a much larger scale.
  • Analytics (OD-04): activity items summed into daily totals by a scheduled Lambda; exported to S3 and queried with Amazon Athena if the volume grows.
  • Not recommended at this scale: a relational database beside DynamoDB, Amazon ElastiCache (it needs a private network for the Lambdas and costs money from day one) or a graph database.

The interactive venue map (VM-01 to VM-15) v5

  • One map record per event. Pins and zones are stored together as one DynamoDB item per event. v6 Positions and zone boxes are saved as fractions (0 to 1) of the layout image's width and height, so they line up at any screen size.
  • Drawn as a layer over the image. The layout image is the background; pins and zone boxes are drawn on top as SVG (react-native-svg with gesture handling in the app, plain SVG in the Next.js portal), so zoom, pan and taps need no map service.
  • Stall listings as their own items. Each stall's description and items (name and price in LKR) are stored with the stall and the vendor; stalls that use profile prices point to the profile instead. Search by item (VM-11) filters one event's listings, a few hundred at most, so it needs no search service. v6
  • Editing. The web portal is the main place to draw zones and place pins; the app allows small edits on the day. Republishing (VM-03) sends the new map to everyone viewing it through a GraphQL subscription. v6
  • Offline in the app (VM-15, Could). The app saves the map record, layout image, times and announcements on the device when an event is opened, and refreshes them when the signal returns.
  • No new backend service. The venue maps module stores and serves the map; the stall listings sit in the hiring module next to stalls (HR-10 to HR-12).

13. Risks and mitigations

The biggest risks are scope, getting all four parties on board at once, and trust: fake organisers, fake reviews and unsafe contact.

RiskImpactMitigation
The scope is large for an SDGP timeline: four core modules plus add-ons, on mobile and web. v2HighBuild thin end-to-end slices first (publish an event, hire a provider, upload a venue map). Keep Should and Could items out until the Musts work, and review scope every sprint.
Cold start: organisers won't join without consumers and providers, and the reverse.HighPilot with one or two partner organisers and the vendors they already use. Seed communities by city and topic.
Fake organisers: fake events, or stall fees and bookings agreed in the app and never honoured. v2HighVerify organisers before they open stalls or hire (AC-08), let admins unpublish reported events (AD-03), and keep the booking and stall record as evidence for disputes (AD-04).

Personal data such as NIC scans and messages creates legal obligations.HighCollect the minimum, restrict documents to admins, and follow the PDPA (section 7).
Without ticketing, organisers still need another site to sell tickets, so Tezera adds a tool instead of replacing one. v2MediumLink to ticket sales from the event page (EV-01), lead with hiring, reputation and community, which rivals lack (section 5), and bring ticketing first after the ATP.
Fake or revenge reviews. v2MediumReviews only from completed bookings, confirmed stalls or 'Going' marks made before the event, a rank score that damps small samples, and admin moderation.
