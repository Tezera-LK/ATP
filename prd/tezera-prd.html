Product Requirements Document · SDGP Agile Team Project

tezera

Version 7 · updated 20 November 2026 · first written 30 September 2026 by Navindu Ranwatte

Tezera is a mobile app and web portal that brings Sri Lanka's event organisers, vendors, freelancers and consumers onto one platform. Organisers promote events, hire vendors and freelancers, fill stalls and share venue maps; consumers discover events, mark that they're going and meet up through forums. The ATP release covers all four core modules: events and promotion, the hiring marketplace, community and venue maps. Ticketing and entry come first after the ATP, followed by real-money payments; during the ATP, hiring and stall payments run on a payment gateway's sandbox only. Premium accounts come in a later version.

1. Problem and opportunity

We believe organising a large event in Sri Lanka means juggling separate channels, and no single place connects organisers, vendors, freelancers and attendees. These are hypotheses, not findings: the team will test them during discovery, before the design is final.

PartyProblem we believe they faceHow we will check
OrganisersVendors and freelancers are found through word of mouth and social media, with little trustworthy reputation data. Ticketing, promotion and attendee updates each need a different tool.Interviews with event organisers
VendorsHearing about events that need their services, or open stall slots, depends on personal contacts. Good work at one event does not build a visible track record.Survey of stall operators and agencies
FreelancersThe same as vendors. Individuals also find it harder to compete with agencies without portfolios and reviews in one place.Survey of photographers, videographers, DJs and MCs
ConsumersEvent details are spread across organisers' social pages, venue layouts are rarely shared ahead of time, and there is no easy way to find others who are going.Consumer survey
v3 Survey results are in. Surveys of 26 attendees, 7 organisers and 13 vendors and freelancers ran from 4 to 8 October 2026. The findings, and the PRD changes they suggest, are in the survey analysis for the group to review.
v5 All twelve recommended changes were accepted on 15 October 2026 and are now in the requirements below (see "What changed in version 5").

The opportunity: one platform where each party's activity feeds the others. Events create demand for vendors and freelancers. Completed jobs and attended events create reviews. Forum activity builds an audience for the organiser's next event.

2. Goals and non-goals

The ATP release should prove that one platform can carry the work around an event: promotion, hiring, venue maps and community. Ticketing follows as the first phase after the ATP.

Goals

  • Organisers publish events and promote them with posts and reels; consumers mark events as 'Going' or 'Interested'. v2
  • Organisers hire vendors and freelancers by direct booking or job posts, and fill stalls by invitation or open application. v6 Deposits, instalments and stall fees can be paid through a test payment gateway, to prove the flow before real money.
  • Reputation is visible and earned: reviews run in four directions and feed category ratings, a rank score, tier badges and leaderboards.
  • Consumers plan their visit with the organiser's venue map and meet others through event boards, communities and meetups.
  • Conversations between organisers, consumers, vendors and freelancers happen in the app instead of across separate tools.

Non-goals for the MVP

  • Ticketing and entry: ticket tiers, reserved seats, free RSVP tickets, QR tickets and gate scanning, and refunds. This is the first phase after the ATP (6.3). v2
  • Real money in the app. v2 v6 Hiring and stall payments run on a payment gateway's sandbox only (HR-14, HR-17 to HR-20), and no real money moves. Going live needs the legal and tax steps in section 14. Ticket payments stay with ticketing.
  • Premium accounts for VIP or fast-sale tickets (later version).
  • A 3D view of the venue; the interactive 2D map (6.6) covers finding stalls, stages and facilities. Google Maps directions to the venue are still under team discussion (section 14). v2 v5
  • Posts or reels from vendors, freelancers or consumers in the main feed. The feed is for organisers; providers show their work in portfolios.
  • More than one role on a single account.

Proposed rank score (a starting point to tune with real data):

Rank = 0.5 · R̂ − 14 + 0.2 · V + 0.2 · B + 0.1 · S R̂ = v·R + m·Cv + m

R is the average star rating and v the number of reviews. C is the average rating across the category, and m is a minimum review count (proposed: 5). Together they stop a single five-star review from topping a leaderboard. V is the review count v, and B the number of completed jobs, each scaled 0-1 against the category leader. S is the share of booking requests answered. For organisers (proposed), B counts completed events and R combines ratings from consumers and providers. C and the scaling use all organisers, and S is the share of consumer chats and Q&A questions answered.

6.6 Venue maps

In the MVP, organisers upload a floor plan of the venue, drop labelled pins on it and draw zones such as a food court; consumers open it from the event page. v5 The map is interactive: tapping a zone lists its stalls, and each stall shows what it sells and at what price, so consumers can find a stall by what they want. Ranking, the feed and search v4 v7

  • Rank score (RV-04, HR-01). A rank_score column on provider and organiser profiles. NestJS recalculates it in the same transaction that writes a review, or hides one in moderation (RV-07).
  • Leaderboards and search order (RV-06, HR-01). An index on category, district and rank_score, so the top of a board is one indexed query and provider search comes back sorted by rank. If boards get slow, a materialized view refreshed on a schedule replaces the live query (proposed).
  • Tier badges (RV-05). Worked out with the rank score from the score and completed jobs or events, and stored on the profile.
  • Promotion feed (EV-03, EV-04). Feed items live in DynamoDB. The feed is built when the user opens it: posts from each organiser they follow, read by author and date, merged newest first and paged with cursors. Only organisers post, so there are few authors. If follow lists grow long, switch to writing each new post into its followers' feeds (fan-out on write) from DynamoDB Streams.
  • Trending posts. A scheduled Lambda scores recent posts by engagement that fades with age, (likes + 2·comments + 3·shares) / (age in hours + 2)^1.5, every 15 minutes, and stores the top list (formula and interval proposed; tune with real data).
  • Likes and view counts. Each like is its own DynamoDB item keyed by post and user, written with a condition so nobody likes twice. Counts on the post are kept with atomic counter updates; view counts are added in batches (proposed). These counts also feed the organiser analytics (OD-04).
  • Search (EV-05, HR-01). PostgreSQL full-text search with pg_trgm trigram indexes on event and provider names, plus filters for category, district, date, price, free events and age rating. Trigram matching tolerates small typos.

Payments in the sandbox (HR-14, HR-17 to HR-20) v6 v7

  • Money goes straight to the payee. Each provider, and each organiser who takes stall fees, connects their own PayHere merchant account (HR-17). Tezera opens that account's checkout, and the money settles to them. Tezera never collects, holds, splits or pays out money. If it did, Sri Lanka's Central Bank would most likely class it as a money or value transfer service, which needs registration and a company with at least LKR 15m in capital. PayHere also lists multi-vendor platforms as unsupported, and Stripe is not available in Sri Lanka. Sources are in the research briefs.
  • Flow. v7
    1. NestJS creates a payment row in PostgreSQL (status pending) for an instalment, a stall fee or a cancellation fee.
    2. The client opens the payee's PayHere checkout: the hosted page on the web, the Flutter SDK in the app.
    3. PayHere posts the result to the API's notify route.
    4. NestJS checks PayHere's signature (md5sig) and updates the payment once per order ID, so a repeated notice changes nothing.
    5. In the same transaction it marks the instalment as received (HR-15) or the stall as paid (HR-18), then queues receipts through SQS.
  • Secrets. Each payee's PayHere merchant secret is stored in AWS Secrets Manager (proposed). It is never sent to the apps.
  • Provider interface. One TypeScript interface (start checkout, verify notice, refund) with PayHere as the first implementation. Genie Business (sandbox credentials on request) or WEBXPAY can be added behind the same interface.
  • Commission. Receipts show Tezera's commission as its own line (HR-20), worked out but not charged. At a real launch it would be invoiced to the payee separately, not deducted from the payment.
  • Labelling. Every payment screen and receipt says 'Test payment, no real money' until the group decides to go live.

12. Assumptions and dependencies

The ATP release rests on organisers, providers and consumers all using one new platform; the ticketing phase will also rest on organisers accepting platform tickets at the gate and on a local payment gateway. Check these assumptions early. v2

Assumptions

  • Consumers have smartphones with mobile data (and, for ticketing, can pay online).
  • Everyone can like, comment on and share organiser posts, even though only organisers publish to the feed.
  • Stall vendors are businesses; freelancers do not take stalls.
  • Hiring and stall fees are paid outside the app in the MVP, so disputes about them rely on the booking record as evidence. v6 Sandbox payments (HR-14, HR-18) only test the flow; no real money moves.
  • v6 Providers and organisers can get a PayHere sandbox account themselves; for real money, each would need a live merchant account (a business and a bank account; PayHere also accepts unregistered sellers with other evidence).

  • v2 'Going' marks are honest enough to unlock reviews, with moderation (RV-07) catching abuse.
  • For ticketing: organisers will accept the platform's QR and online tickets at the gate. The team notes that some Sri Lankan organisers already accept online and QR tickets.

Before taking real money v6

From the payments research in the research briefs. These need a lawyer or accountant; none blocks the sandbox.

  • Does opening checkouts on each payee's own merchant account keep Tezera outside the Central Bank's money or value transfer service rules? The regulations list no exemption for agents.
  • Will PayHere, Genie Business or WEBXPAY let a platform open checkouts on its users' own accounts? PayHere lists multi-vendor platforms as unsupported.
  • Who withholds the 5% tax on freelancer fees, and who issues the invoice: the organiser or Tezera?
  • Which VAT registration threshold applies now (sources disagree), and when would Tezera have to register for VAT and SSCL?
  • The PDPA basis for keeping payment records in AWS Mumbai, and the duties that start on 1 January 2027, such as a data protection officer.
  • Do the Consumer Affairs Authority's e-commerce rules apply to bookings between organisers and providers, especially when the organiser is an individual?
  • Does the Central Bank's 2026 payments-law reform add a licence for payment aggregators?
